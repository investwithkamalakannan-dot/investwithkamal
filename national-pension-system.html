At a glance

The National Pension System is a defined-contribution pension system regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). Contributions are invested according to the subscriber’s recorded pension-fund and asset-allocation choices, with withdrawals and exit governed by current NPS rules.

Retirement-focused structure

NPS is designed for long-term retirement accumulation. The eventual corpus depends on contributions, investment performance, charges and the rules applicable at the time of withdrawal or exit.

Investment choices

Subscribers can choose among pension funds and permitted asset-allocation approaches under the current NPS framework. The available choices and limits can change, so current PFRDA and NPS Trust information should be checked before making or changing an allocation.

Active and automatic allocation

NPS can offer choices where the subscriber selects asset allocation and alternatives where allocation follows a pre-defined age-based approach. Each approach has a different risk profile.

Contributions and account servicing

Regular contributions, nominee details, contact information, pension-fund choice and transaction records should be maintained carefully. The Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN) is central to the subscriber record.

Exit rules and tax treatment

NPS exit, withdrawal and annuity rules are regulatory matters and may change. Tax treatment also depends on current tax law and the subscriber’s circumstances. Always verify the latest PFRDA and tax provisions rather than relying on an old illustration.

Frequently asked questions

What is NPS?

The National Pension System is a defined-contribution pension system regulated by PFRDA and designed to support retirement accumulation.

Who regulates NPS in India?

NPS is regulated and supervised by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA).

What is a PRAN?

PRAN stands for Permanent Retirement Account Number and is the unique identifier associated with an NPS subscriber account.

How are NPS contributions invested?

Contributions are invested by the selected pension fund according to the asset-allocation choice recorded for the subscriber and the applicable PFRDA framework.

What asset classes are used in NPS?

The standard NPS framework includes permitted asset classes such as equity, corporate debt and government securities, with allocation choices subject to current rules.

What is Active Choice in NPS?

Active Choice allows a subscriber to choose allocation among permitted asset classes within the limits specified under the current NPS rules.

What is Auto Choice in NPS?

Auto Choice uses a pre-defined allocation pattern that changes with age according to the selected life-cycle option and current NPS rules.

Can NPS rules change over time?

Yes. Eligibility, investment choices, withdrawals, exits and other operational rules can be revised by the regulator. Current official information should be checked before acting.

Does NPS guarantee a fixed retirement return?

No. NPS returns depend on market-linked investment performance, contributions, allocation and charges; a fixed return should not be assumed.

What should I review before opening or changing NPS?

Review the retirement objective, contribution capacity, asset allocation, risk, liquidity constraints, nomination, charges and current withdrawal/exit rules.
