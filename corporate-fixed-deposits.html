At a glance

A corporate fixed deposit is a term deposit accepted by an eligible company or NBFC under applicable rules. It can offer periodic or cumulative interest, but repayment depends on the issuer and product terms. Corporate FDs should not be treated as identical to insured bank deposits.

How corporate FDs work

Money is placed with the issuer for a chosen tenure under the stated rate and payout option. Depending on the product, interest may be paid periodically or accumulated until maturity. Rates and terms vary by issuer and tenure.

Issuer and credit risk

The investor is exposed to the issuer’s ability to meet interest and principal obligations. Credit ratings can be one input, but a rating is an opinion at a point in time and not a guarantee of repayment.

Corporate FD versus bank FD

A corporate FD issued by a company or NBFC is different from a bank fixed deposit. DICGC deposit insurance applies to eligible deposits with insured banks; deposits mobilised by NBFCs are not covered by DICGC.

Liquidity and premature withdrawal

Premature withdrawal may be restricted, unavailable for an initial period or subject to a lower interest rate or other terms. Investors should keep emergency money separate and check the exact premature-closure rules.

What to compare

Review issuer type, credit rating, tenure, interest rate, payout option, premature withdrawal terms, taxation, nomination, application process and maturity instructions. A higher stated rate should be considered together with higher issuer risk.

Frequently asked questions

What is a corporate fixed deposit?

A corporate fixed deposit is a term deposit accepted by an eligible company or NBFC under the applicable regulatory framework and the issuer’s stated terms.

Is a corporate FD the same as a bank FD?

No. They can differ in issuer, regulatory framework, risk and deposit-insurance protection.

Are NBFC fixed deposits covered by DICGC?

No. DICGC states that deposits mobilised by NBFCs are not covered under its deposit insurance scheme.

Is the interest rate guaranteed for the chosen tenure?

The contracted rate and payout terms apply subject to the deposit terms, but the investor still carries issuer credit risk and must read the product documents.

What does a credit rating mean?

A credit rating is an assessment of credit risk by a rating agency at a point in time. It can change and is not a guarantee of repayment.

Can I close a corporate FD before maturity?

Premature withdrawal depends on the issuer’s terms and applicable rules. Restrictions or reduced interest may apply.

What is cumulative interest?

In a cumulative option, interest is generally added according to the product terms and paid with the principal at maturity rather than paid periodically.

What is a periodic interest payout option?

Some deposits may offer monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annual interest payouts depending on the issuer’s product structure.

What should I check before investing in a corporate FD?

Check the issuer, rating, tenure, rate, payout option, liquidity, premature withdrawal rules, taxation and maturity instructions.

Can a higher interest rate mean higher risk?

A higher rate can be associated with higher issuer or product risk. Rate alone should not be the deciding factor.
