At a glance

Health insurance is designed to help manage eligible medical expenses subject to the policy wording. Individual, family floater and senior citizen plans can differ significantly in sum insured, waiting periods, co-payments, room-rent rules, exclusions, network arrangements and claim procedures.

Individual health insurance

An individual policy provides a separate sum insured for the insured person, subject to the policy terms. It can be considered when a person wants coverage that is not shared with other family members.

Family floater health insurance

A family floater generally provides a common sum insured that eligible family members share. The suitability of a floater depends on family size, ages, medical history and how much shared coverage may be needed.

Senior citizen health insurance

Plans designed for older adults may include different entry conditions, medical underwriting, waiting periods, co-pay requirements and premium structures. Policy wording should be reviewed especially carefully for pre-existing conditions and sub-limits.

What to compare

Compare the sum insured, waiting periods, exclusions, room-rent or category restrictions, co-pay, disease-specific sub-limits, restoration features, network hospitals, pre/post-hospitalisation coverage and claim process. Do not compare only on premium.

Claims and disclosures

Accurate proposal-form disclosure is important. Claims are assessed according to policy terms, medical records, exclusions and insurer procedures. Keep policy documents, health records and insurer contact details accessible.

Frequently asked questions

What is health insurance?

Health insurance is a contract under which the insurer covers eligible medical expenses according to the policy terms, limits, exclusions and claim conditions.

What is individual health insurance?

Individual health insurance provides coverage for one insured person with a separate sum insured, subject to policy terms.

What is a family floater policy?

A family floater generally provides one shared sum insured for the covered family members. The available balance can be used by eligible members according to policy conditions.

What is senior citizen health insurance?

Senior citizen health insurance refers to policies designed for older adults. Features such as underwriting, co-pay, waiting periods and exclusions can differ by insurer and plan.

What is a waiting period?

A waiting period is a specified period during which certain claims or conditions are not covered, as described in the policy wording.

What is co-pay in health insurance?

Co-pay is the portion of an admissible claim that the policyholder must bear. The percentage and applicable situations are defined by the policy.

Why should I check room-rent limits?

Room-rent restrictions can affect the amount payable for hospitalisation and may influence associated claim calculations, depending on the policy wording.

Does health insurance cover every medical expense?

No. Coverage is subject to the policy wording, exclusions, waiting periods, limits, medical necessity and other terms.

Should I disclose existing medical conditions?

Yes. Proposal forms should be completed accurately and honestly. Material non-disclosure can affect underwriting and claim decisions.

What should I compare before buying health insurance?

Compare coverage, exclusions, waiting periods, co-pay, sub-limits, network access, claim process, service quality and premium rather than focusing on premium alone.
