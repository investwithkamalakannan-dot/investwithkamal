At a glance

Mutual funds pool money from investors and invest according to a stated scheme objective. You can invest periodically through a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) or make a one-time lump-sum investment. Scheme categories differ in asset mix, volatility, liquidity and intended use, so the category should be understood before any transaction.

Ways to invest

A SIP invests a chosen amount at regular intervals, while a lump-sum investment is a one-time purchase. Neither method guarantees a return. The choice between SIP and lump sum depends on cash flow, the purpose of the money, time horizon and comfort with market movements.

Mutual fund categories

AMFI groups mutual fund schemes into broad categories such as equity, debt, hybrid, solution-oriented and other schemes including index funds/ETFs and fund of funds. ELSS is an equity-linked tax-saving category. Each category has a different portfolio structure and risk profile.

What to review before investing

Start with the goal, time horizon, liquidity need and ability to tolerate temporary losses. Then review the scheme objective, riskometer, portfolio, benchmark, expense ratio, exit-load terms and current scheme documents. Avoid selecting a scheme only because of recent returns.

Transaction and servicing support

Distribution support can include KYC guidance, SIP registration, one-time purchases, folio and nomination processes, bank-detail updates, statement support, redemption requests and other permitted mutual fund servicing activities.

Risk comes before return

Mutual fund values can rise or fall. Equity-oriented categories can be volatile, debt funds can carry interest-rate and credit risk, hybrid funds combine multiple asset classes, and index funds track the risk of their underlying index. Read the current scheme-related documents carefully.

Frequently asked questions

What is a mutual fund investment?

A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors and invests it according to the scheme objective. The value of units changes with the underlying portfolio and market conditions.

What is the difference between SIP and lump sum?

SIP is a method of investing at regular intervals, while lump sum is a one-time investment. Both are only methods of investing; the underlying mutual fund still carries market risk.

What are equity mutual funds?

Equity mutual funds primarily invest in equity and equity-related instruments. They generally have higher short-term volatility and are usually evaluated with a longer investment horizon in mind.

What are debt mutual funds?

Debt mutual funds primarily invest in fixed-income instruments such as government securities, corporate bonds and money-market instruments. They can still carry interest-rate, credit and liquidity risks.

What are hybrid mutual funds?

Hybrid funds combine more than one asset class, commonly equity and debt. The risk level depends on the actual asset allocation and the scheme category.

What is an index fund?

An index fund is a passive mutual fund that aims to track a specified market index. Its portfolio and performance are linked to that index, subject to tracking difference and expenses.

What are solution-oriented mutual funds?

Solution-oriented schemes are categories designed around specific long-term objectives such as retirement or children-related goals and may have product-specific restrictions.

What is ELSS?

ELSS is an equity-linked savings scheme category with a statutory lock-in. Any tax benefit depends on the tax regime and law applicable to the investor, so current tax rules should be checked.

Are mutual fund returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and cannot be guaranteed merely because the investment is made through SIP, lump sum or a particular category.
